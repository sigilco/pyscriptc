# Draft recorded decision: the new gate, streaming serializer and TIR_HASHES probe

Status: DRAFT for owner sign-off, 2026-11-02. On approval this is the decision row that unblocks roadmap R3+ gate movement (and, at R5, the `IR_HASHES.json` retirement); until then the new gate stays beside the old one and no frozen file changes. Input: `.agents/plans/2026-11-02-ir-rearchitecture-roadmap.md` and `.agents/notes/2026-11-02-ir-rearchitecture-reassessment.md`.

## Decision (proposed)

The compiler's IR canonicity gate is replaced by a streaming-serializer golden gate: SHA-256 streamed directly over the canonical IR in node id order (later, schema field order for the typed node) with the payload never materialized as a dict, baseline `probes/TIR_HASHES.json`, regenerated by the existing run-twice-`cmp` ritual unchanged. `IR_HASHES.json` and the `canonical.py` dict-shape rules are retired only by the R5 retirement decision, on the criteria the roadmap records: the new gate byte-stable A/B on the full corpus through the migration and green two weeks, or having caught one real nondeterminism. Until that decision the new gate runs beside the old one and every frozen gate file stays untouched.

The full serializer spec, the probe file format, and the regen ritual are in the "Proposed" section of `.agents/docs/spec/ir-canonicity.md`; that section is the artifact carrying the detail and this row is the approval that activates it.

## Why

The old gate's oracle is a test oracle over the old representation's determinism: 1.4MB of hand-regenerated digests whose only failure mode is "the IR you already know changed", regenerable in an afternoon under the 2026-10-22 ritual, ~30 repins and counting. A gate that cannot describe a new representation cannot guard the R3 strangler: the migration needs a gate whose digests move only through a recorded ritual while corpus outputs stay byte-identical. The streaming shape makes the typed-IR canonicity rules (field order in the serializer, not dict-shape enforcement) trivially true instead of policed, which is the reassessment's correction to the prior plan's "free canonicity" claim.

The bootstrap receipt (2026-11-02, `work/tirgate`) is the evidence the byte-identity premise holds before any IR exists: the streaming serializer over today's canonical JSON stream reproduces today's `json.dumps` bytes on the full visible corpus (13,692 probes, 0 byte mismatches), and the A/B run-twice ritual is green (`cmp` byte-identical). Cost accepted: the streaming path is about 1.9x the wall time of the dict path (4.31s vs 2.31s over the corpus) in exchange for never materializing the payload, which matters at the arena/MIR scale and not at today's.

## Alternatives rejected

- Keep `IR_HASHES.json` through the migration: the digests describe the dict IR being deleted; every slice would move a 1.4MB frozen file it did not meaningfully change.
- Content hashes of source files instead of IR digests: gates on the input, not the compiler's output; catches none of the nondeterminism the gate exists to catch.
- A different digest (xxhash, blake2b): closed by section 3 of ir-canonicity.md; SHA-256 is stdlib and hardware accelerated at these sizes, one digest everywhere.

## What reopens the decision

A corpus-size change that makes the 1.9x serializer cost material, a nondeterminism class the node-id/field-order order does not cover, or the R5 retirement criteria failing to hold within two weeks of the migration's end.
